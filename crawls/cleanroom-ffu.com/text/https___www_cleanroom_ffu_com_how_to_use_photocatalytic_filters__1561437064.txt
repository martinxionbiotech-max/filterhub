##### How to Use Photocatalytic Filters
Core Principle: Photocatalytic filters (typically coated with Titanium Dioxide, TiO₂ ) use UV light to trigger a chemical reaction that breaks down airborne pollutants (VOCs, bacteria, viruses, odors) into harmless CO₂ and H₂O. UV light is essential – without it, the filter is ineffective.
### Key Usage Steps
- Install in Compatible Devices: Designed for air purifiers , HVAC systems , or car air cleaners with built-in UV lamps (UV-A or UV-C).
- Ensure airflow passes through the filter surface.
- Activate UV Light: UV light must be ON during operation. Check device indicators.
- Never look directly at UV lights (eye/skin hazard).
- Replace UV bulbs per manufacturer’s schedule (typically every 1–2 years).
- Correct Installation: Follow airflow direction arrows on the filter.
- Ensure a tight seal to prevent air bypass.
- Dry Cleaning Only (Critical!): NEVER wash with water – it destroys the TiO₂ coating.
- Avoid chemicals or abrasive tools.
- Gently vacuum with a soft brush or wipe with a dry microfiber cloth monthly.
- Timely Replacement: Replace every 1–2 years (or per device alerts).
- Degraded performance or physical damage = immediate replacement.
- Combine with Other Filters: Works best paired with: • Pre-filter (traps large particles/dust). • HEPA filter (captures fine particles like PM2.5).
- Alone, it does not trap dust/pollen .
### Important Notes:
- Effectiveness: Targets gases (formaldehyde, odors) and microbes, not particulate matter.
- Safety: Byproducts are generally safe (CO₂/H₂O), but buy certified products.
- Patience: Decomposition isn’t instant – run the device continuously in polluted spaces.
### Summary:
Install correctly → Keep UV lights functional → Clean monthly (dry only!) → Replace when needed. Always pair with mechanical filters (Pre/HEPA) for full air purification.
