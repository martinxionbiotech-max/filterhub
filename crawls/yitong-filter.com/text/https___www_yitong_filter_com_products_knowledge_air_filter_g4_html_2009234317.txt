# Air Filter G4: A Complete Guide to G4 Panel Air Filters
Clean air is essential for commercial buildings, industrial facilities, and ventilation systems. Choosing the right air filter helps reduce airborne dust, protect HVAC equipment, and improve the performance of an air filtration system.
An Air Filter G4 is a common choice for general-purpose air filtration. It is widely used as a pre-filter in heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems. By capturing larger airborne particles, a G4 filter helps prevent dust from reaching higher-efficiency filters and other equipment.
For businesses looking for a reliable G4 air filter manufacturer, understanding filter efficiency, construction, applications, and replacement requirements is important.
In this guide, we will explain what a G4 air filter is, how it works, where it is used, and how to choose the right G4 panel air filter for your application.
## What Is an Air Filter G4?
An Air Filter G4 is a general-purpose air filter classification traditionally associated with the EN 779 standard. G4 filters were commonly used for coarse particle filtration in ventilation and air conditioning systems.
Today, ISO 16890 is the current international standard commonly used to classify general ventilation air filters. Depending on its tested performance, a filter previously described as G4 may fall into a particular ISO 16890 coarse-filter category. However, the two classification systems are not directly interchangeable, so the actual test results should be checked when selecting a replacement filter.
A G4 air filter is designed primarily to capture larger airborne particles, including:
- Dust and dirt
- Fibers and lint
- Larger pollen particles
- Other coarse airborne contaminants
It is generally used as a first-stage filter rather than as the final filtration solution for applications requiring very clean air.
For example, in a multi-stage HVAC system, a G4 panel filter can capture larger particles before the air passes through a medium-efficiency or high-efficiency filter.
This arrangement helps protect downstream filters and supports stable system operation.
## How Does a G4 Air Filter Work?
A G4 air filter removes airborne particles as air passes through its filter media. The media captures particles through a combination of mechanisms, including interception, inertial impaction, and, depending on the material, other particle-capture effects.
The filter is installed in an air handling unit, ventilation duct, or filter housing. As the ventilation system operates, incoming air passes through the filter media, where some of the airborne contaminants are retained.
Over time, dust accumulates on the filter surface and within the media. As the filter loads with dust, its pressure drop generally increases.
When the pressure drop reaches the recommended replacement limit, the filter should be replaced to maintain suitable airflow and avoid unnecessary energy consumption.
The main purpose of a G4 air filter is not to remove every fine particle. Instead, it provides economical first-stage filtration and helps extend the service life of downstream filters.
## Key Features of a G4 Panel Air Filter
G4 panel air filters are popular because they offer a practical balance of filtration performance, cost, and ease of maintenance.
### 1. Effective Coarse Particle Filtration
A G4 panel filter is designed for general ventilation applications where the primary goal is to remove larger airborne particles.
It helps reduce dust entering air handling equipment and provides an initial stage of filtration before more efficient filters.
### 2. Multiple Frame Options
Depending on the application, G4 air filters can be manufactured with different frame materials, including galvanized steel, aluminum, and plastic.
The correct frame material depends on operating conditions, installation requirements, durability expectations, and budget.
### 3. Different Filter Media
Common media options include synthetic fiber and fiberglass, depending on the filter design and intended application.
Synthetic media is often selected for general HVAC filtration because of its practical handling characteristics and suitability for disposable filters.
The final media selection should consider airflow, dust-loading capacity, moisture conditions, and the manufacturer's tested performance data.
### 4. Low Initial Resistance
A properly selected G4 filter can provide relatively low initial pressure drop at its specified airflow.
Lower resistance can help reduce the energy required to move air through a ventilation system. Actual performance depends on the filter dimensions, media, construction, and operating airflow.
### 5. Easy Installation and Replacement
Panel air filters are available in a range of standard and custom dimensions. They can be installed in compatible filter frames, air handling units, and ventilation equipment.
Correct sizing is essential to prevent air from bypassing the filter around its edges.
### 6. Cost-Effective Maintenance
G4 air filters are commonly used as pre-filters because they can capture larger dust particles before these contaminants reach more expensive downstream filters.
A suitable pre-filtration system can help reduce maintenance requirements and improve the overall operating efficiency of an HVAC installation.
## Common Applications of G4 Air Filters
G4 air filters are used in many commercial and industrial ventilation systems.
### Commercial Buildings
Offices, shopping centers, hotels, and other commercial buildings require continuous ventilation. G4 panel filters help capture coarse dust from incoming air and support routine HVAC maintenance.
### Industrial HVAC Systems
Factories and industrial workshops often generate dust and airborne fibers. G4 filters can be used as a first filtration stage in compatible ventilation systems.
The filter should be selected according to the type and concentration of contaminants present in the working environment.
### Air Handling Units
Air handling units (AHUs) distribute conditioned air throughout buildings and facilities. A G4 air filter can serve as a pre-filter to reduce the amount of coarse dust entering the unit.
This can help protect internal components and downstream filtration stages.
### Commercial Ventilation Systems
Fresh-air systems in commercial and public buildings often use coarse filters to capture larger particles before the air enters the building.
G4 filters may be suitable where the required filtration performance and applicable regulations allow their use.
### Manufacturing Facilities
Some manufacturing environments require multiple filtration stages to protect equipment and maintain air quality.
A G4 panel filter can provide initial dust removal before air passes through higher-efficiency filters. More demanding applications, such as pharmaceutical production, electronics manufacturing, and cleanrooms, may require additional filtration stages and a carefully designed filtration system.
### Pre-Filtration for Higher-Efficiency Filters
One of the most important uses of a G4 air filter is protecting downstream filters.
In a typical multi-stage system, the filtration arrangement may include:
- First stage: G4 coarse air filter
- Second stage: Medium-efficiency air filter, selected according to the required performance
- Final stage: HEPA filter, where high-efficiency particle removal is required
The exact combination depends on the system design, air quality requirements, and operating conditions.
## G4 Air Filter vs. Other Air Filter Grades
Different filter grades are designed for different filtration requirements. The following comparison provides a general guide to their traditional applications.
TABLE:
  Filter type | Typical role | Common applications
  G3 air filter | Basic coarse filtration | General ventilation
  G4 air filter | Coarse pre-filtration | HVAC systems and AHUs
  M5 air filter | Higher filtration performance in systems using legacy EN 779 terminology | Commercial and industrial ventilation
  F7 air filter | Higher-efficiency filtration in legacy EN 779 terminology | Applications requiring improved fine-particle removal
  H13 HEPA filter | High-efficiency particle filtration | Cleanrooms, pharmaceutical facilities, and other controlled environments

Important: G3, G4, M5, and F7 are legacy EN 779 classifications. When purchasing new filters, compare the applicable ISO 16890 classification, test conditions, and performance data rather than assuming that old and new grades are equivalent.
H13 HEPA filters are classified under a different standard and serve a different filtration purpose from G4 pre-filters.
A G4 filter should not be used as a substitute for a HEPA filter when high-efficiency filtration is required.
## How to Choose the Right G4 Air Filter
Selecting the right G4 air filter involves more than choosing a filtration grade. Buyers should consider the following factors before placing an order.
### 1. Filter Dimensions
Measure the required length, width, and depth. Even small dimensional differences may affect installation and sealing.
If the filter is replacing an existing unit, confirm whether the dimensions refer to the nominal size or the actual external dimensions.
Custom sizes may be available for equipment with non-standard filter housings.
### 2. Airflow Requirements
Check the ventilation system's required airflow and the filter's rated airflow.
A filter operating outside its specified conditions may experience excessive pressure drop or provide performance different from the expected result.
### 3. Initial Pressure Drop
Initial pressure drop indicates the resistance to airflow when the filter is clean under specified test conditions.
Compare pressure-drop data at the same airflow when evaluating different products. A lower initial pressure drop can be beneficial, but it should not be considered separately from filtration performance and dust-holding capacity.
### 4. Filter Media
Choose filter media based on the working environment.
Synthetic media may be suitable for many general HVAC applications. Other media may be preferred for particular dust characteristics, moisture conditions, or equipment requirements.
Always confirm material compatibility with the intended operating conditions.
### 5. Frame Material
Common frame options include galvanized steel, aluminum, and plastic.
Consider corrosion resistance, mechanical strength, weight, installation requirements, and total operating cost when choosing a frame.
### 6. Operating Environment
Temperature, humidity, dust concentration, and possible chemical exposure can influence filter life and material selection.
If the filter will be exposed to unusual conditions, discuss these requirements with the manufacturer before ordering.
### 7. Replacement and Maintenance
A practical replacement plan helps maintain airflow and filtration performance.
Replacement intervals should be based on actual operating conditions, pressure-drop limits, manufacturer recommendations, and maintenance requirements rather than a fixed schedule alone.
### 8. Supplier Experience and Quality Control
For commercial and industrial buyers, a reliable G4 air filter supplier should provide clear product specifications, consistent manufacturing quality, and responsive technical support.
Before purchasing, ask for relevant information such as:
- Filter dimensions and construction details
- Filter media and frame materials
- Initial pressure drop at the specified airflow
- Applicable filtration classification and test data
- Available standard and custom sizes
- Packaging, lead time, and shipping arrangements
These details make it easier to compare suppliers and select a filter that meets your system requirements.

## Frequently Asked Questions About G4 Air Filters
### 1. What is a G4 air filter used for?
A G4 air filter is primarily used for coarse particle filtration in ventilation and HVAC systems. It captures larger airborne particles and is commonly installed as a pre-filter before higher-efficiency filters.
### 2. Is a G4 air filter a HEPA filter?
No. A G4 air filter is a coarse pre-filter, while a HEPA filter is designed for much higher particle-removal efficiency under a separate classification system. They serve different purposes in an air filtration system.
### 3. What is the difference between a G4 and an F7 air filter?
G4 and F7 are classifications from the former EN 779 standard. G4 filters were used for coarse filtration, while F7 filters provided higher filtration performance. When comparing modern products, check their ISO 16890 classifications and supporting test data.
### 4. Can a G4 air filter be used in an AHU?
Yes. G4 filters are commonly used in air handling units as an initial filtration stage, provided that their dimensions, airflow capacity, pressure drop, and filtration performance meet the system requirements.
### 5. How often should a G4 air filter be replaced?
There is no universal replacement interval. The frequency depends on dust concentration, operating hours, airflow, and the filter's pressure-drop limit. Regular inspection and pressure-drop monitoring can help determine when replacement is necessary.
### 6. Are G4 panel air filters available in custom sizes?
Many manufacturers offer custom-sized G4 panel filters. Buyers should provide the required dimensions, frame material, media requirements, and operating conditions when requesting a quotation.
### 7. What information is needed to get a G4 air filter quotation?
To obtain an accurate quotation, provide the following details:
- Required length, width, and depth
- Estimated order quantity
- Required airflow
- Preferred frame and filter media
- Operating temperature and humidity, if relevant
- Delivery destination
- Any required classification or test documentation
Providing complete specifications helps the manufacturer recommend an appropriate product and prepare a more accurate quotation.
